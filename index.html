Understanding Bayes' Theorem

Click each card to flip and reveal the definition
📊
Prior Probability P(A)
The initial probability of an event before any new evidence is considered. It represents your existing belief based on previous knowledge or data.
🔄 Click to flip
🎯
Likelihood P(B|A)
The probability of observing the evidence (B) given that the hypothesis (A) is true. It measures how well the evidence supports the hypothesis.
🔄 Click to flip
🔄
Posterior Probability P(A|B)
The updated probability of the hypothesis after taking into account the new evidence. This is the result of applying Bayes' Theorem.
🔄 Click to flip
📐
Bayes' Theorem
P(A|B) = [P(B|A) × P(A)] / P(B). It describes how to update probabilities based on new evidence.

Practice Problem

Test your understanding with a real-world Bayes problem

Medical Diagnosis Problem

A rare disease affects 1 in 1,000 people (0.1% prevalence). A diagnostic test has:

  • • Sensitivity (True Positive Rate): 95%
  • • Specificity (True Negative Rate): 99%

Question: If a person tests positive, what is the probability they actually have the disease?

Solution

Step 1: Define variables

P(Disease) = 0.001  |  P(¬Disease) = 0.999

P(Positive | Disease) = 0.95  |  P(Positive | ¬Disease) = 0.01


Step 2: Apply Bayes' Theorem

P(Disease | Positive) = [0.95 × 0.001] / [0.95 × 0.001 + 0.01 × 0.999]

Step 3: Calculate

Numerator = 0.00095

Denominator = 0.00095 + 0.00999 = 0.01094


Final Answer: 0.00095 / 0.01094 = 8.7%

💡 Even with a 95% sensitive test, the positive predictive value is only 8.7% for a rare disease!
